JUNE'S JOURNEY — FEATURE

Player Profile

← Back to June's Journey

Player Profiles allow the player to establish their identity and showcase themselves within the game's social system. While the feature might seem simple, I had to additionally consider Facebook integration, privacy concerns, and handling bad actors, since, for the first time, our players were being exposed to each other.

Feature Breakdown

Social Impetus

Based on the learnings from Pearl's Peril, and inspired by games like World of WarCraft, June's Journey had a long-term vision of providing players with a social space that would serve to provide a reason to keep engaging with the game even after content releases stopped. This would be important to maintaining long term retention.

As part of our roadmap for the social features we needed to first allow our players to build an identity. I spent a good deal of time considering how we would broach this concept of "identity" to the player, since, up to this point, the player had always played the role of June! Now, for the first time, the player would have an identity detached from June.

Theming

An early decision to make was how the system would be themed. Do we keep it diegetic or a more detached look, like our Settings menu? With UI, I explored both approaches in early sketches. I felt the passport theme was a great fit. Passports would be era appropriate and play into the aspect of the player visiting the island of other players (another feature that we were planning to implement), and it fit with June's globtrotting adventures.

We knew this approach would be more art intensive, though, but after discussions with stakeholders we agreed it would be in the scope for the feature and so we moved forward with the passport theme. Beyond just the looks, there were several decisions to make on the technical and functional implementation.

Name

Naming yourself is always a critical moment in onboarding players to the social setting. As part of the feature FTUE, the player could set their name (or leave it as the randomly generated name).

I wanted to avoid feel-bads for the player when it came to naming themself, if they chose to engage with the system, and so I didn't want there to be name collision. This would ensure a painless flow through the Profile feature FTUE, if the player chose to engage with it.

I did want to avoid players quickly changing names, though, or potentially impersonating others. In service of that, I added a cost to changing your name beyond the first name change. This would cost hard currency to prevent potential abuse and to make social identity more static, so identity could be relied upon when making social connections.

Avatars

For our avatars, I wanted to provide a variety of different portraits that would cover different demographics, so any player could find a choice that might approximate how they wanted to present themselves. Avatars provided us with a chance to reuse assets, as we already has several character faces and expressions that were used in our dialog scenes.

The assets would need some conversion, though, and I wanted to be able to suppor the addition of new avatars over the air, so a new section was added to our data config sheet to support avatars, and new set of assets added to the project.

Besides just the in-game portraits, players could also connect to Facebook and use their Facebook profile image, but it would only be visibile to Facebook friends.

Facebook Support

Before the social features, we already had support to connect to Facebook so players could save their progress and transfer it between devices. Facebook also had several groups devoted to June's Journey, and Wooga had a long history of Facebook integration. All these reasons played into the desire to support Facebook identity through our own profile system as well.

Connecting to Facebook would interact with the player name and avatar in different ways.

Privacy

Privacy was identified as a critical component to the feature early on. Up to this point, players had played disconnected from each other, with no other identity than being June. Once social features were unlocked though, players would be exposed to each other, and you couldn't opt out completely. To help ease the player into this change, I wanted to provide two layers of anonymity to the player.

The first layer was the default avatar and name given to all players. If a player didn't wish to engage further with the system, they could leave these as they were without any further interaction. The second layer was Facebook connection. Even if a player connected to Facebook, they could choose whether they wanted to show their Facebook profile picture or not. And even if the player decided to use their Facebook profile picture, it would still only be visible to people they were connected to through Facebook itself. The third and final layer was being able to disable custom names of other players. With this turned on, a player would only see the default name of any other player.

While some of these layers could lead to confusion as one player might appear differently to other players, this would only be an issue if it occured between members of the same Club. This would provide an opportunity to engage with the club's chat system and ultimately deepen social bonds, so I found it to be an acceptable approach.

Defaults

Because I wanted to make social engagement as optional as possible for a first time experience, defaults would be needed if the player chose not to customize their profile. I also wanted to keep in mind what the early player experience would be like if many weren't customizing their profile. One of the earliest exposures players would have to each other would be through the Leaderboard that would appear after playing a hidden object scene.

As part of the profile system, I also worked with the dev team to create a random name generator. This name would be permantly attached to the player's account and would serve as their display name for players that did not want to see custom names.

All players would start with the same avatar, however, which would be June. From the beginning of the game, before the player gets into any of the social aspects, the player sees June associated with their player level. This helped establish the initial immersion to the island and the story progression, but once social features were unlocked, we would then allow the player to break away from June and establish their own identity. This would mean that early players would see many other June's on the leaderboards or other social features. While this did make players seem a bit samey, it was actually a way to encourage players to change their profile image, as they would immediately distinguish themselves among the default avatars.
